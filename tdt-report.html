Time Dilation Theory, tested

TDT counts candles from a swing and treats the numbers 7, 13 and 21 as decision points. This page takes that literally and asks the only question that matters: does a leg turn at 7 more often than it turns at 6 or 8?

What the material actually specifies

Three counting modes, read off the Advanced Counting Examples slides. A count starts at a swing candle numbered 1 and runs forward.

classic
Every candle takes the next number.
wick
A candle takes the next number only if it expands the leg — its high (up leg) exceeds the last numbered candle's high. Otherwise it is marked x and skipped.
body
The same test on the body extreme, max(o,c) up / min(o,c) down.

Contraction counting therefore always reaches a given number at or later than classic does. That is exactly the disagreement the slides draw: on the EURUSD M1 example the candle classic calls 13, contraction-by-wick calls 12.

The two models

Model #2
Counts on D1, execution on H1. [1-7] = continuation of the previous fractal; [1-13] = reversal inside the previous fractal.
Model #3
The same count at three nested swing degrees, giving a signature. The conclusion slide grades 13-7-7 and 7-13-7 good, 7-7-7 acceptable, and refuses anything containing a 21.

Leg census

Before any claim can be tested there has to be something to count. This is where legs actually terminate, per mode — and how many land near a key count at all.

The hazard curve — the central test

Given a leg that has already reached count n, how often does it turn there? If 7 is special the curve spikes at 7. If it runs smooth through 7, the number is doing no work: legs simply have a length distribution and 7 sits inside it.

Bar height is the turn rate at that count. The pale bars behind are how many legs were still running — a spike off a handful of legs is noise, and the table below gives the sample for each.

Does it matter where the count starts?

The same legs, renumbered from an origin displaced a few candles off the swing. The leg and its endpoint are held fixed, so the only thing varying is the alignment TDT says is meaningful. For classic this is the neighbour test by another route — displacing the origin just renumbers every candle — so it earns its keep on the contraction modes, where a displaced origin also changes which candles get skipped.

Model #2 — does the read pay?

Every leg terminating on 7 or 13, traded against the leg that just counted. Entry is the open after the swing could have been confirmed, stop beyond the terminal extreme, target a multiple of risk.

Model #3 — testing the conclusion slide

The slide's own claim, made falsifiable: signatures it calls good against the ones it calls not advised. If the grading carries information, ok beats not advised.

Reward multiple sweep

Both models across the reward-to-risk grid. A result that only exists at one multiple is a result about that multiple.

How to read this, and what it cannot tell you

  • Entry timing is the load-bearing assumption. A swing of strength k is not knowable until k candles later. Entering at the turn itself would manufacture an edge nobody could take, so entry is the open after confirmation.
  • Terminal counts rarely land exactly on 7 or 13. A tolerance snaps a count to the nearest key; every table states the tolerance used. Widen it and more legs qualify but the claim gets weaker.
  • Sample thins fast at 21. Few legs survive that long, so the 21 row is the one most likely to show a large z off almost nothing. Read the at-risk column first.
  • Swing degree is a choice, not a given. Model #3's three degrees are parameters here; the slides draw them by eye.
  • This measures whether the counting numbers predict turns in this data. It says nothing about the discretionary judgement a trader adds on top.
