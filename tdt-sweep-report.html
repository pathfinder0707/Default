TDT — the full search

The first study tested one parameterisation. This searches the whole space — both trade polarities, both signal timings, every counting mode, five swing degrees, four timeframes — selects on , and reports what those choices earned on , which selection never saw.

Protocol

Fixed before looking at any result, because a search this wide will always produce a winner and the only defence is deciding in advance what would count as one.

Two readings the first pass never tried, both defensible from the slides: polarity — the slides call [1-7] a continuation and [1-13] a reversal, which plausibly means a 7 is traded with the leg and a 13 against it, so both directions are searched for both counts. And signal timing — the first pass required a leg to have ended on a key count, which a trader only knows k candles later, whereas a trader watching a count acts when it reaches 7. Reach signals need only the origin pivot.

What the search itself produces

Every configuration scored on train. This distribution is the reason a single good backtest proves nothing: with this many attempts, some t-statistics are large by construction.

Finalists — train against test

One finalist per reading (signal × count × polarity), so a dozen near-identical neighbours of one lucky configuration cannot fill the shortlist. Each was chosen purely on its train t-statistic, then run once on the held-out period.

Bootstrap CI is 95%, percentile method, 2,000 resamples of the test trades. A finalist clears the bar only if its test CI excludes zero and it beats the control below — beating zero alone just re-discovers the trade structure.

The control — does the count add anything?

Fade every leg regardless of its count, same entry, stop, target and timeout. This already has an edge, established in the first study. A TDT configuration has to beat this, not zero.

Test equity

Cumulative R on the held-out period for each finalist that produced trades there.

The count is a time offset

The search's strongest survivor was reach 7, follow on classic counting. Classic counting numbers every candle, so "the count reaches 7" is arithmetically "six candles after the pivot". Tested against a plain origin+6 entry the contrast came out at +0.0000R, t=+0.00 — not near zero, the same trades. Whatever that configuration earns, no counting produced it.

Which makes one question decisive: is offset 6 better than the offsets beside it? If the curve runs smooth through 6, TDT's 7 is decoration on a time delay. Each bar is a fixed-time hold with no stop and no target, so nothing but the signal's directional content survives.

Where the edge actually peaks

For each swing strength k, the entry offset that pays best. Offsets below k are greyed: the pivot is not knowable yet, so they collapse onto k. If the number 7 meant something, the peak would sit on it whatever k is.

What you can actually trade

The rule at the peak, stated plainly: when a swing pivot of strength k confirms, enter at the next candle in the direction of the leg that just completed. Stop beyond the leg's extreme padded by a quarter of its mean candle range, target three times risk, timeout twenty candles. M15, NQ, costs charged, and non-overlapping — one position at a time, which is what a single account can take.

This is a real effect and it is not TDT's. It is momentum after a confirmed pivot, and it decays with every candle of delay — which is why the best count is always k+1, the first moment you are allowed to act.

Fixed-time hold — profit and drawdown

Enter on the signal, hold a set number of candles, exit at the close. No stop, no target, costs charged, and non-overlapping — a new signal is skipped while a position is open, which is what one account can actually trade. Dollars are one NQ contract at $20 a point.

Reading this honestly

  • Costs are charged. Deducted per round turn on every trade, which hurts short timeframes hardest, because the same points are a larger fraction of a smaller stop.
  • Selection is on train only. No test result influenced any choice. Test numbers are therefore usable at face value, subject to the Bonferroni threshold for the number of finalists carried forward.
  • Train-to-test decay is the tell. A configuration whose edge halves out of sample was fitted, not found — even when both numbers are positive.
  • The 100-trade floor excludes configurations too thin to evaluate; it is applied on train, so it cannot leak test information.
  • No walk-forward re-fitting: parameters are chosen once on train and frozen. That is the conservative choice and understates an adaptive system.
