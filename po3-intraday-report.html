The verdict, up front. There is no PO3 edge to utilise. A power of three is not a special block size — the results vary smoothly with size, and non-PO3 values beat every PO3 value. The one setup that showed a strong paper edge (+3.32 pts/trade, t = 9.63) was earning it entirely from an optimistic limit-fill assumption, and goes negative the moment fills are modelled honestly.

What is worth keeping is in sections 1 and 6: a sizing table for setting blocks on your chart, and the reason a fixed point-size decays as volatility rises.

The drift problem

NQ's median RTH range more than doubled over the sample while price rose from 11k to 30k. Range as a percentage of price barely moved. A block size fixed in absolute points therefore means something different every year — and that is fatal for a lattice anchored on absolute price.

YearDaysMedian close Median rangeRange % of price P(range ≤ 243)P(range ≤ 729)

243 contained 73.8% of days in 2023 and only 16.8% in 2026. The same number, the same market, a completely different meaning. Any fixed PO3 you pick is right for about one year.

04

How the edge died

S2 assumed a resting limit fills every time a bar's range touches the level. But the trades that make the money are exactly the ones where price tags the level and reverses — and those are the fills you are most likely to miss, because price never traded through your order. Everyone ahead of you in the queue gets filled; you don't.

So require price to trade a given distance beyond the level before counting a fill. The entry price stays the same. Only the fill assumption tightens.

ConfigPenetrationTrades Win %Exp ptst Total pts

Every single point of required penetration costs roughly 1.2 points of expectancy, decaying monotonically through zero. That is not a strategy with a cost problem — that is the shape of a result made entirely of adverse selection. By 3 points through, all three configurations are negative.

Three points of penetration is not a harsh assumption on an instrument whose 1-minute bars have a median range of 10–20 points. It is roughly what you should assume if you are not co-located and near the front of the queue.

06

What to actually do

Don't trade the PO3 lattice

Nothing here supports it. The lattice phase is irrelevant, no power of three is a peak in any sweep, and the only strategy that looked profitable was extracting its edge from a fill assumption rather than from the market.

If you keep the blocks on your chart, size them like this

  • Scalping, 1–15m: R = 27. Median bar ranges are 10–42 points.
  • 30–60m structure: R = 81.
  • Session / 4h: R = 243 historically, but see below.
  • Containing the whole day in 2026: R = 729. 243 now contains only 16.8% of days.

Better: stop using a fixed number. Set your spacing to roughly 0.5–0.6× the trailing 20-day median RTH range — about 140–200 NQ points at current volatility. That tracks the market instead of drifting away from it. It still is not an edge; it is just the honest way to place lines.

The transferable lesson

This is the most valuable thing in the report. Any backtest where you enter with a limit order at a level will overstate its edge, because it assumes you are filled on exactly the touches that reverse instantly. Before believing any such result, re-run it requiring price to trade 2–3 points through your level. If the edge does not survive that, it was never there. This one lost 100% of its expectancy to that single test.

What would change the answer

  • Real CME futures data. This is HistData's cash Nasdaq-100 CFD, not MNQ. The lattice is anchored on absolute price, so the cash-futures basis shifts which lattice line you are on. The phase null argues that is not what is hiding an effect — every phase behaves alike — but genuine MNQ ticks would close it properly.
  • Tick or depth data. It would replace the fill assumption with an actual queue model rather than a penetration proxy.
  • An execution rule the source document actually specifies. The trade plans are discretionary — "price should aggressively reject", "look for potential gap". What is tested here is the structure those plans stand on, not any one trader's reading of them.
